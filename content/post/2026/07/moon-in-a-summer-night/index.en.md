The DK plant encyclopedia I ordered had arrived. After dinner, the air still held a good deal of the day's heat, so I waited until after 8 p.m. to stroll over to the parcel station.

Just out of my building, heading toward the east gate, I looked up and saw a bright, clear moon perched between the buildings like a lantern at a rooftop corner, spilling its gentle light across the ground. While picking up my parcel, I scrolled through my phone and noticed someone in my Moments was sharing the same view. So afterward I wandered around looking for an open spot to photograph the moon myself.

There's a small creek north of the neighborhood, and it was the first place that came to mind. By the time I got there, the evening breeze had turned pleasantly cool and carried a touch of damp, watery air. I zoomed the lens all the way in, set the ISO to 100, and tried shooting the moon directly.

The breeze was still pretty strong, and the telephoto lens wasn't easy to hold steady. I took a dozen or so shots, but only this one above came out reasonably sharp.

I zoomed out a bit, hoping for a shot of the moon above a rooftop, but no matter how I tried, the moon came out overexposed. I decided to figure out the overexposure problem back home. The mosquitoes by the creek were relentless — in the few seconds it took to press the shutter, several would land on my arm. And I hadn't even worn socks, just sandals. A moment longer and I'd have been a full mosquito buffet.

![](./IMG_20260730_213159_compress2.webp)

I finished with a wide-angle shot, then packed up and hurried home.
