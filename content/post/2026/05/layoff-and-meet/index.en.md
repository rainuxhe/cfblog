A few weeks ago, I heard through the grapevine that there would be a round of layoffs. This week, the layoff list started being announced, and quite a few colleagues made the list. Although I haven't received any notice yet, I can't help but feel anxious.

Today, just after 11 AM, I heard some heavy news — an entire team next to ours was cut as a whole project, and they were told to finish the exit procedures before the end of the day. By the time I left work, the office was practically half empty.

During lunch, I chatted with colleagues about trivial things since we joined the company. Many moments felt like they weren't that long ago, but in reality, they were already several years in the past. We also talked about the job market. The employment situation in Hefei isn't great either — major companies have high barriers to entry, and on top of that, they expect mindless overtime. Working three extra days a week is common, and even weekends are filled with busywork. Smaller companies, on the other hand, often suffer from poor management, where you're just coding to please your boss.

I tossed and turned during my lunch break, unable to sleep, with my eyes closed but my mind racing. Worrying about when I might be laid off, and also contemplating my career.

Counting my internship, I've been in this industry for nearly 8 years. When my freshman advisor said the IT industry had good employment prospects and high salaries, it seemed promising. But by the time I graduated, the job market was already tougher. Over the years, my salary hasn't increased much, and I haven't saved much money — it's all gone into housing and my car. Now, approaching thirty, I've accumulated a bunch of minor ailments. Getting old before getting rich.

I should still think about how to deal with layoffs. Panic aside, life must go on. Here are a few points I've noted down.

---

## Severance Letter and Compensation

- Ask the company to issue a formal **severance letter**, which is typically required when joining the next company. Make sure it states "mutual agreement to terminate" or "contract expiration," and avoid negative wording like "dismissal" or "underperformance." (Writing negative comments also carries risks for the company.)
- The statutory minimum for layoff compensation is **N**. If not given 30 days' notice, it should be **N+1**. If the +1 is missing, you can negotiate with the company.
- If labor arbitration is needed, keep records proving your employment, such as time logs and attendance records. (Do not retain confidential data to avoid disputes.)

## Social Insurance

- Confirm until when the company will pay social insurance, **and ask them to pay for the current month**. According to social insurance law, the company should pay for the current month regardless of how many days you worked. If you don't plan to join another company immediately, ask if the company can pay for an additional month. (If you're laid off near the end of the month, it's worth asking.)
- If the gap exceeds one month, **it's best not to let medical insurance lapse**, otherwise you won't be able to use medical reimbursement during the gap. Log in to the "National Social Insurance Service Platform" or search for "Electronic Social Security Card" via Alipay/WeChat to make payments. As a self-employed individual, you can only pay for pension and medical insurance.
- Pension insurance is cumulative. Missing a month or two won't have a major impact — it will only slightly reduce your future pension, not cancel or forfeit eligibility.
- If you need full five-insurance coverage and a long gap, consider registering as a self-employed individual to pay enterprise employee social insurance. Do not use proxy payment or contribution arrangements without a genuine employment relationship, as this carries legal risks.

## Housing Provident Fund

- A lapse in the housing provident fund only affects provident fund loans. Once the loan is approved, a lapse doesn't matter as long as you make monthly payments on time.
- If you plan to apply for a housing provident fund loan soon, it's advisable to continue paying during the gap period yourself.

## Unemployment Benefits

- Unemployment benefits are a basic living allowance provided by the state for those who **lose their jobs not by their own choice**.
- Medical insurance is covered by the unemployment fund during the benefit period.
- You need to have paid unemployment insurance for at least one year and have lost your job involuntarily. Being laid off qualifies.
- If the severance letter states "resignation for personal reasons," you cannot receive unemployment benefits.
- Standards vary by region; the amount is generally 70%–90% of the local minimum wage. The duration ranges from a minimum of 3 months to a maximum of 24 months, depending on cumulative contribution time.
- Register for unemployment on the **National Social Insurance Service Platform** or through **Electronic Social Security Card**.

## Interviews and Joining the Next Company

- Contact former colleagues and supervisors in advance — you'll need them for reference checks.
- When asked why you left, just say it was due to business restructuring.
- Ask about the compensation structure. A low base salary with high performance pay is generally a red flag.
- Pay attention to the social insurance and provident fund contribution ratios. In Hefei, the maximum is 12%. If it's too low, be cautious. Some companies may contribute at the minimum rate during the probation period and adjust after formal employment.
- If you have connections or reliable intel, learn as much as you can about the next company's work environment to avoid stepping into a trap.

## Other Thoughts

- When layoffs start happening around you on a large scale, don't fall into wishful thinking. Prepare in advance. Organize your notes, fill in gaps in your tech stack, update your resume, and browse job apps. If your current company is unbearable, don't wait for a severance package — if you see a good opportunity, just leave.
- I've always taken technical notes at work, focusing less on specific configurations and more on logic and thought processes. This is work experience and accumulation. No matter how layoffs happen, this is the most crucial asset to rely on.
- Things you never had time for because of work — you can do them after leaving. Life isn't all about work. Spend more time with family, take care of yourself, experience the world more, observe the details of life, and reduce internal friction.
- New companies typically require a medical checkup report from the last six months. If you haven't had one, get a thorough checkup. If any issues are found, fix them promptly. Health is the foundation of life.
- Life inevitably has setbacks; bumps along the road are normal. Getting laid off is just that — take a seat, relax, and move on.
- If you have no financial pressure, consider taking it easy — travel, for example. Every time I thought about quitting before, I said I'd travel afterward, but I mostly just stayed at home, consumed by internal friction. Thinking back, it wasn't worth it. I'm not here to save the world, so why be so hard on myself?
