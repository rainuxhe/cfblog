After my two right-side wisdom teeth emerged, I kept biting the inside of my cheek, and recently they started aching more often, so I decided to just have them pulled.

## The Procedure

1. The day before (June 5), I made an online appointment at the Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery department of the nearby Anhui Medical University-affiliated dental hospital.
2. June 6 was a Saturday. I went to the hospital in the afternoon. There's e-bike parking at the north gate. I printed a receipt at the self-service machine on the first floor (if you've already paid online, this might not be necessary).
    1. Since you can only eat liquid foods for the first 24 hours after the extraction, it's wise to eat a little beforehand to stave off hunger.
3. I took the elevator to the 5th floor of the outpatient building, scanned my QR code at the check-in kiosk in the lobby, and waited for my number to be called.
4. After examining me, the doctor said both right-side wisdom teeth were poorly positioned and that tooth #6 was already decayed, so he ordered an X-ray on the first floor.
5. Paid for the X-ray at the first floor using medical insurance — just gave my ID number.
6. Checked in by swiping my ID card at the kiosk outside the imaging center.
7. Had the X-ray done as instructed by the technician.
8. Went back to the 5th floor, checked in again, and waited for my number.
9. The doctor reviewed the X-ray and said the upper right tooth had fully erupted, but the main problem was the lower right one. It had only half emerged, and its roots were dangerously close to the nerve. There was a risk of nerve damage during extraction, which could take months to heal and might leave the right side numb in the meantime. I also asked about fillings and orthodontics and learned that getting a filling might require removing the adjacent tooth — best to clarify the order with the dentist beforehand.
    1. The doctor didn't mention the upper tooth being crooked, only that if the lower one was removed, the opposing upper one should come out too. I'd felt it before though — it did protrude a little outward, though the X-ray didn't show it.
    2. The doctor said the left-side wisdom teeth were also a bit misaligned. I'd sensed that before too, but as long as my jaw isn't skewed like Jim Carrey's comedy faces, it doesn't really bother me. So the plan was: take out the two on the right first and decide about the left side later.
10. The doctor asked if I wanted to go ahead — if so, sign the consent form and do it right then.
11. Confirmed. The extraction began.
12. Started with the lower tooth. After the local anesthetic injection, I waited three or four minutes until even my lips went numb, then the doctor got to work. The wisdom tooth was growing upwards and not too tilted, but it still took some effort. The doctor said the root had a little hook, making it slightly tricky.
13. Next, the upper tooth. Another injection, but the wait was shorter — likely just topping up the anesthesia. The doctor still had to work at it because the upper tooth was rather wide.
14. After both teeth were out, the doctor explained that the lower gum had already been inflamed, so it bled a fair bit, but by the time the upper tooth was removed, the bleeding had stopped. Both teeth came out intact, with no root fragments left behind.
15. The doctor told me to go pay on the first floor and also bring up the anesthetic and other supplies. My head was probably a bit numb — I remembered the payment but forgot to bring the supplies. The doctor said it was fine though, no need for another trip.
16. The doctor asked me to take a photo of the aftercare notice posted nearby and went over the key points verbally. He also listed the medications to buy: cephalosporin and ornidazole as anti-inflammatory antibiotics, and ibuprofen if the pain got really bad. Ice packs if swelling occurred. Note the time, then remove the hemostatic gauze from your mouth by yourself after half an hour.
17. Left the hospital and bought the medication at a pharmacy.
18. Arrived home half an hour later and removed the gauze from my mouth.
19. Went back to the hospital a week later to have the stitches removed.

## Aftercare Instructions

1. Do not eat or drink anything for the first 2 hours after the extraction. That includes water — let the wound go through its initial healing phase undisturbed.
2. After 2 hours, you may have cool, liquid foods, but do not use a straw (the suction could dislodge the blood clot). Apply ice packs frequently. Do not touch the wound with your hands. Minimize talking.
3. On the day of the extraction: no brushing, no rinsing, no hot/hard/spicy foods, no strenuous exercise, and no hot showers.
4. If there is significant bleeding or large blood clots within 2–24 hours, apply pressure to the wound with clean gauze and return to the hospital immediately.
5. If the area is still markedly swollen and painful 3–4 days later, return to the hospital promptly.
6. Return in a week to have the stitches removed.

## Costs

- Registration: 15 CNY
- X-ray: 40 CNY (medical insurance)
- Extraction: 1,013.42 CNY (medical insurance — 325.17 from the pooled fund, 688.25 from the personal account)
- Medication: 79.70 CNY (medical insurance)
- **Total: 1,148.12 CNY**

## Medication

- Antibiotics: cephalosporin + ornidazole — taken for 4 days
- Painkiller: ibuprofen — only when the pain becomes unbearable
- Ice pack: apply to the cheek if swelling occurs

## Recovery Journal

- **~2.5 hours post-op:** The anesthesia wore off and the wound began to hurt — a painful dullness with some residual numbness lingering. I searched Zhihu for similar experiences; apparently lower jaw extractions generally hurt more. Took an ibuprofen capsule. Didn't feel much relief. Just had to tough it out.
- **Day 0, evening:** Had soup-soaked rice for dinner. Once cooled, it was quite thick, so I thinned it with some cooled boiled water. Chewed the first few bites once or twice, then worried food debris might get into the wound, so I swallowed half a spoonful at a time. Ate painfully slowly.
- **Day 0, night:** Couldn't brush my teeth or take a hot shower. Didn't rinse my mouth either. But it was summer and I'd sweated quite a bit, so I filled a basin with cold water and wiped myself down with a towel.
- **Day 1:** Rinsed my mouth in the morning (no brushing) and spat out some yellowish fluid. After a few rinses things went back to normal. Ate only congee all day. Got terribly hungry in the afternoon and realized I could add shredded meat and leafy greens to the congee. Went for a walk after dinner — it was overcast and windy, felt a bit cold. Came back feeling like I might have caught a chill and had a low fever. That night I brushed only the left side of my mouth, took a hot shower, and went to sleep.
- **Day 2:** Monday, back at work. Felt listless — not sure if it was the chill or the medication side effects. Could still only brush the left side; the right side of my mouth felt really unclean. The wound still ached dully.
- **Day 3:** Felt a bit more energetic, though the wound still ached. I knew I shouldn't talk much, but work communication was unavoidable. After talking too much, my right cheek really hurt. I'd frozen an ice pack in the fridge and tried applying it that evening, placing it directly on my jaw without wrapping it in a towel.
- **Day 4:** Regretted the casual ice pack experiment the night before. Woke up around 6 a.m. with a dull, cold ache in my cheek — as if I'd been exposed to a draft. Lesson: don't ice it if there's no swelling or fever, and absolutely never apply ice directly to the skin. In the morning I tried eating some steamed buns and eggs. Opening my mouth wide felt rather strange, so I took small nibbles and ate slowly. When brushing, I'd dip the toothbrush in clean water and lightly clean the right side of my mouth.
- **Days 5–6:** Mostly ate bland, mild foods.
- **Day 7:** Went to the hospital to have the stitches removed. Although they were dissolvable sutures, the thread ends didn't seem to have been tied properly — they kept catching when I bit down and were a hassle to clean. Since the doctor had said to return in a week, I just went ahead. Stitch removal took less than a minute and wasn't charged extra. Had a proper feast that day.
